Full Meaning and Definition of Kharcha

Kharcha (खर्चा) is an extremely common Hindi word meaning — any money you pay out to buy something, use a service, or fulfil a need or want.

In simple terms: every time money leaves your wallet or bank account — that is Kharcha.

  • Paid rent to the landlord → Kharcha
  • Bought groceries from the shop → Kharcha
  • Paid the electricity bill → Kharcha
  • Ordered food from Zomato → Kharcha
  • Filled petrol in your vehicle → Kharcha
  • Took a Netflix subscription → Kharcha
  • Paid your monthly EMI → Kharcha

The sum of all of these is your Kul Kharcha (Total Expenses) for the month.

How Many Types of Kharcha Are There?

Kharcha is divided into two main types. Understanding this is crucial because it tells you exactly where you can cut back:

🏠
Essential Kharcha
Fixed Expenses / Needs
Expenses for your basic necessities that you cannot live without. These stay roughly the same every month and must be paid no matter what.
Examples: Rent, electricity, food, medicine, transport, EMIs
🎬
Non-Essential Kharcha
Variable Expenses / Wants
Expenses for your desires and lifestyle choices — not strictly necessary but enjoyable. These can be reduced or eliminated when needed without affecting your basic quality of life.
Examples: Eating out, OTT, shopping, movies, outings
💡 Key Insight

When money is tight, always cut Non-Essential Kharcha first. Essential expenses must be paid, but non-essential spending can be reduced by 20–30% without any real sacrifice — saving you thousands every month.

How to Calculate Your Monthly Kharcha

Calculating your total monthly kharcha is straightforward — just follow these 4 steps:

  • 1

    Open Your Bank & UPI Statement

    Go to your bank app, PhonePe, Google Pay, and credit card and open last month's transaction history. Every debit entry is part of your kharcha. This is the most accurate record of where your money actually went.

  • 2

    Note Down Cash Expenses Too

    Tea, auto rides, vegetable vendors — these cash kharcha items don't show up in your bank statement. Try to recall them and write them down separately. These are the most overlooked expenses but they add up fast.

  • 3

    Sort Into Categories

    Group every expense into the categories above — food, housing, transport, shopping, etc. This immediately reveals which category is consuming the most money and where your kharcha can be reduced most easily.

  • 4

    Add Everything Up = Total Kharcha!

    Add the totals from all categories. That final number is your monthly kul kharcha (total expenses). Subtract it from your income — what remains is your savings. If the result is zero or negative, reduce your kharcha immediately.

How to Reduce Kharcha – 6 Easy Ways

Reducing your kharcha doesn't mean living poorly — it means spending money in the right places and cutting the rest:

  • 1

    Separate Essential from Non-Essential Kharcha

    For every expense, ask: "Can I genuinely not live without this?" Rent and medicine — essential. Netflix and eating out — non-essential. Cutting just 20–30% of your non-essential kharcha can free up thousands of rupees every month.

  • 2

    Pay Attention to Small Daily Kharcha

    A ₹50 tea, a ₹100 auto, a ₹150 snack — these feel tiny but add up to ₹3,000–5,000 in kharcha every month. Simply writing them down daily makes you naturally more careful and your kharcha starts dropping on its own.

  • 3

    Cancel Unused Subscriptions

    Go through your phone and list every active subscription — Netflix, Spotify, gym, cloud storage. How many do you actually use every week? Cancel anything you're not using. This single step can reduce monthly kharcha by ₹500–2,000 with zero effort.

  • 4

    Cook at Home More Often

    Ordering from Zomato or Swiggy costs 3–5 times more than cooking at home. Eating home-cooked meals 5 days a week reduces your monthly food kharcha by ₹2,000–4,000 — and your health improves as a bonus.

  • 5

    Shop With a List

    Going to a supermarket without a list almost always results in buying things you didn't need. Make a shopping list before you go and stick to it. This small habit saves ₹500–1,500 in kharcha every month without any sacrifice.

  • 6

    Track Your Kharcha Every Week

    Creating a budget isn't enough — you need to follow it. Set aside 10 minutes every Sunday to check your weekly spending. PaisaTrack automatically calculates your total kharcha with category charts so tracking takes no effort.

Common Mistakes People Make with Kharcha

  • ❌

    Not tracking cash kharcha: UPI and card expenses appear in statements, but cash spending — tea, auto, vegetable vendors — gets forgotten. These add up to ₹2,000–4,000 in hidden kharcha every month.

  • ❌

    Not counting EMIs as kharcha: Phone, bike, and personal loan EMIs are 100% part of your monthly kharcha and must always be included in your total.

  • ❌

    Forgetting irregular kharcha: Birthday gifts, festival shopping, annual insurance renewals — divide them by 12 and include that monthly portion in your kharcha budget.

  • ❌

    Ignoring credit card kharcha: Credit card spending feels like "future money" so people overspend. But the bill always arrives. Track credit card kharcha in real time, not just when the statement comes.

  • ❌

    Only reviewing kharcha at month-end: By then it's too late to fix anything. Review your kharcha every week so you can catch overspending early and adjust before the month ends.

Pro Tips to Keep Kharcha Under Control

  • Save first, spend what remains: The moment your salary arrives, transfer at least 20% to savings immediately. Then spend from what's left. "I'll save whatever is left" almost always results in saving nothing.
  • Write down your kharcha every day: Before sleeping, note every expense for the day — in a notebook or PaisaTrack. This one habit makes you automatically more careful with money.
  • Follow the 30-day rule for big purchases: Before spending more than ₹2,000 on anything, wait 30 days. If you still want it after 30 days, buy it. Most impulse kharcha disappears with this rule.
  • Scroll your UPI history every week: Open PhonePe or Google Pay and go through your transactions. This simple habit builds powerful awareness about where your kharcha is going.
  • Set a monthly kharcha limit: At the start of each month, decide: "My total kharcha will not exceed ₹X this month." A specific number makes you think twice before every non-essential purchase.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is Kharcha in English?›
Kharcha means Expense or Expenditure in English. In everyday conversation it also means Spending or Cost. Kul Kharcha = Total Expenses. Ghar ka Kharcha = Household Expenses. Roz ka Kharcha = Daily Expenses.
How many types of Kharcha are there?›
Kharcha is of two types: Essential Kharcha (Needs/Fixed) — rent, electricity, food, medicine. And Non-Essential Kharcha (Wants/Variable) — eating out, OTT, shopping, entertainment. Always cut non-essential kharcha first when saving money.
How much Kharcha should I have per month?›
According to the 50/30/20 rule, your total kharcha should not exceed 80% of your income. For ₹25,000 income, ideal total kharcha = ₹20,000 and savings = ₹5,000.
What is the easiest way to reduce Kharcha?›
Cancel unused subscriptions, cook at home more, shop with a grocery list, track your daily spending, and review your weekly kharcha every Sunday. PaisaTrack's free app makes all of this automatic.
Should savings be included in Kharcha?›
No. Savings and kharcha are completely separate. Total Kharcha = Needs + Wants + EMIs. Savings = Income − Total Kharcha. Including savings inside your kharcha total gives you a wrong picture of your spending.
What is the difference between Kharcha and Budget?›
A Budget is your plan — how much kharcha you intend to have before the month starts. Kharcha is reality — how much you actually spent. A budget keeps your kharcha under control, and tracking kharcha tells you whether your budget worked.
