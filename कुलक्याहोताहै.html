Full Meaning and Definition of "Kul"

"Kul" (कुल) is a very common Hindi word meaning the sum of all things combined. It is the equivalent of "Total" or "Grand Total" in English — the final number you get when you add everything together.

For example:

  • If you earn ₹20,000 from your job and ₹5,000 from freelance work — your Kul Aay (Total Income) = ₹25,000
  • If you spent ₹7,000 on rent and ₹4,000 on food — your Kul Kharcha = ₹11,000 + other categories
  • If ₹5,000 is left at the end of the month — your Kul Bachat (Total Savings) = ₹5,000

In everyday life, the word "Kul" appears everywhere — a shop bill, a school report card, a bank statement. Wherever you see "Kul," it always means the grand total of everything combined.

The 3 Main Types of "Kul" in Personal Finance

In personal finance and budgeting, "Kul" appears most frequently in three places:

💰
Kul Aay
Total Income (कुल आय)
The complete amount of money you earn in a month from all sources — salary, freelance, business, rental income, interest, and any other earnings combined.
Kul Aay = Salary + All Other Income
💸
Kul Kharcha
Total Expenses (कुल खर्चा)
The complete amount of money you spend in a month across all categories — rent, food, bills, transport, shopping, entertainment, and EMIs combined.
Kul Kharcha = Sum of All Expenses
🏦
Kul Bachat
Total Savings (कुल बचत)
The money that remains after subtracting all expenses from your total income. This is the single most important indicator of your financial health every month.
Kul Bachat = Kul Aay − Kul Kharcha

What is Kul Aay (Total Income)?

Kul Aay (Total Income) is the complete amount of money you earn in a month — from every source, no matter how small.

Kul Aay includes:

  • Monthly Salary: The fixed pay from your job
  • Freelance or Part-time Earnings: YouTube, content writing, design, tutoring, etc.
  • Business Income: Profit from a shop, online business, or side hustle
  • Rental Income: Rent received if you have a property on lease
  • Interest Income: Interest earned on bank FDs or savings accounts
  • Dividends or Investment Returns: Returns from mutual funds or shares
💡 Important Distinction

Kul Aay (Gross Income) and Shuddh Aay (Net Income) are different. Kul Aay = your total earnings before any deductions. Shuddh Aay = what actually reaches your bank account after TDS, PF, and professional tax deductions. When building a personal budget, always use your Net Income (Shuddh Aay) — the amount you actually receive.

What is Kul Kharcha (Total Expenses)?

Kul Kharcha (Total Expenses) is the complete amount of money you spend across all categories in a single month. It includes everything:

  • 🏠 Housing: Rent, electricity, water, gas, internet
  • 🍛 Food: Groceries, vegetables, milk, eating out, Swiggy/Zomato
  • 🚗 Transport: Petrol, bus/metro pass, auto, Ola/Uber
  • 👕 Shopping: Clothes, shoes, household items, online orders
  • 🎬 Entertainment: OTT platforms, movies, games, outings
  • 💊 Health: Medicines, doctor visits, gym membership
  • 💳 EMIs: Phone, bike, or personal loan monthly installments

Add all of these up and you get your Kul Kharcha (Total Expenses) for the month.

What is Kul Bachat (Total Savings)?

Kul Bachat (Total Savings) is the money that remains after you subtract all your expenses from your total income. It is the single most honest indicator of your financial progress each month.

📐 The Core Financial Formula
Kul Bachat = Kul Aay − Kul Kharcha
₹8,000 = ₹30,000 − ₹22,000
If Kul Bachat is zero or negative — your expenses are exceeding your income. Time to act immediately.

For a healthy Kul Bachat, the 50/30/20 rule is the most popular guideline: save 20% of your Kul Aay, spend 50% on needs, and 30% on wants.

Key Financial Formulas Using "Kul"

  • Kul Bachat = Kul Aay − Kul Kharcha
  • Kul Kharcha = Needs + Wants + EMIs
  • Kul Aay = Salary + Freelance + All Other Sources
  • Savings Percentage = (Kul Bachat ÷ Kul Aay) × 100
  • Expense Percentage = (Kul Kharcha ÷ Kul Aay) × 100
  • Ideal: Kul Kharcha ≤ 80% of Kul Aay

Common Mistakes When Calculating "Kul"

  • ❌

    Forgetting cash expenses in Kul Kharcha: UPI and card transactions appear in statements, but cash payments — auto rides, tea stalls, vegetable vendors — are often forgotten. These can total ₹2,000–4,000 per month, making your Kul Kharcha look lower than it really is.

  • ❌

    Not including all income sources in Kul Aay: Many people count only their main salary. But freelance earnings, interest, cashback, and other side income are also part of Kul Aay and must be included for an accurate picture.

  • ❌

    Keeping EMIs separate from Kul Kharcha: All EMIs — phone, bike, personal loan — are 100% part of your monthly Kul Kharcha and must always be included in your total.

  • ❌

    Including savings inside Kul Kharcha: Savings and expenses are two completely different things. Kul Bachat = Kul Aay − Kul Kharcha. Adding savings into expenses throws off your entire calculation.

  • ❌

    Missing annual or irregular expenses: Insurance renewals, car servicing, festival shopping — these don't come every month but they are part of your annual spending. Divide them by 12 and include that monthly portion in your Kul Kharcha every month.

Tips to Keep Your Kul Aay, Kharcha & Bachat on Track

  • Note your Kul Aay at the start of every month: Write down every income source and the amount. This tells you exactly how much money you have to work with before any spending begins.
  • Record expenses daily: Enter your spending into PaisaTrack every day — the app automatically calculates your Kul Kharcha by category with charts at month-end.
  • Save first, spend what remains: The moment your salary arrives, immediately transfer your savings target (at least 20% of Kul Aay) to a separate account. Then spend from what's left.
  • Check your Kul Kharcha every week: Don't wait until month-end. A quick 10-minute Sunday review catches overspending early enough to course-correct.
  • Follow the 50/30/20 rule: Allocate 50% of Kul Aay to needs, 30% to wants, and 20% to Kul Bachat. This simple formula keeps all three numbers healthy.
  • Track your savings percentage monthly: Calculate (Kul Bachat ÷ Kul Aay) × 100 every month. Above 20% — excellent. Below 10% — time to reduce Kul Kharcha.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What does "Kul" mean in English?›
Kul (कुल) means Total or Grand Total in English. It is the sum of all things combined. In personal finance: Kul Aay = Total Income, Kul Kharcha = Total Expenses, Kul Bachat = Total Savings.
What is Kul Aay in English?›
Kul Aay means Total Income in English. It is the complete amount of money earned from all sources in a month — salary, freelance, business profits, rental income, interest, and any other earnings all added together.
What is the difference between Kul Aay and Shuddh Aay?›
Kul Aay (Gross Income) = your total earnings before any tax or deductions. Shuddh Aay (Net Income) = what actually reaches your bank account after TDS, PF, and professional tax deductions. Always use Net Income when building a personal budget.
How do you calculate Kul Bachat?›
The formula is: Kul Bachat = Kul Aay − Kul Kharcha. Example: if total income is ₹30,000 and total expenses are ₹22,000, Kul Bachat = ₹8,000. As a percentage: (8,000 ÷ 30,000) × 100 = 26.6% savings rate — excellent!
How much of my income should I save every month?›
The 50/30/20 rule recommends saving at least 20% of your Kul Aay every month. For ₹30,000 income that means ₹6,000 per month. If 20% feels too much right now, start with 5–10% and increase gradually.
What is the difference between Kul Kharcha and a Budget?›
A Budget is your plan — what you intend to spend before the month begins. Kul Kharcha is reality — what you actually spent during the month. A budget helps keep Kul Kharcha under control, and tracking Kul Kharcha shows whether your budget plan worked.
