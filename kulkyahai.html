Full Meaning and Definition of "Kul"

"Kul" (कुल) is a very common Hindi word meaning — the sum of all things combined. It is the equivalent of "Total" or "Grand Total" in English — the final number you get when you add everything together.

Everyday examples of "Kul" in action:

  • Shop bill at the bottom — "Kul = ₹850" → total of all items purchased
  • School report card — "Kul Marks = 480/500" → total of all subjects combined
  • Bank statement — "Kul Kharcha = ₹18,000" → total of all expenses for the month
  • Salary slip — "Kul Salary = ₹35,000" → basic + HRA + allowances all added together

In personal finance, "Kul" is used in three key places: Kul Aay, Kul Kharcha, and Kul Bachat. Knowing and tracking all three can completely transform your financial life.

The 3 Main Types of "Kul" in Finance

In personal finance, "Kul" is used most in these three places:

💰
Kul Aay
Total Income (कुल आय)
The complete amount of money earned in a month from all sources — salary, freelance, business income, rental income, interest, and any other earnings combined.
Kul Aay = Salary + All Other Income
💸
Kul Kharcha
Total Expenses (कुल खर्चा)
The complete amount spent across all categories in a month — rent, food, bills, petrol, shopping, entertainment, and EMIs all combined.
Kul Kharcha = Sum of All Expenses
🏦
Kul Bachat
Total Savings (कुल बचत)
The money that remains after subtracting all expenses from total income. This is the single most important indicator of your financial health every month.
Kul Bachat = Kul Aay − Kul Kharcha

What is Kul Aay (Total Income)?

Kul Aay (Total Income) is the complete amount of money you earn in a month from all sources.

  • Monthly Salary: Fixed pay from your job
  • Freelance Earnings: YouTube, content writing, design, tutoring, etc.
  • Business Income: Profit from a shop or online business
  • Rental Income: Rent received from a property you lease out
  • Interest Income: Interest earned on bank FDs or savings accounts
  • Investment Returns: Returns from mutual funds or share market
💡 Important Distinction

Kul Aay (Gross Income) = total earnings before any tax or deductions.
Shuddh Aay (Net Income) = what actually reaches your bank after TDS and PF deductions.
When building a personal budget, always use Net Income (Shuddh Aay) — the amount you actually receive.

What is Kul Kharcha (Total Expenses)?

Kul Kharcha (Total Expenses) is the complete amount you spend across all categories in a single month:

  • 🏠 Housing: Rent, electricity, water, gas, internet
  • 🍛 Food: Groceries, vegetables, milk, eating out, Swiggy/Zomato
  • 🚗 Transport: Petrol, bus/metro pass, auto, Ola/Uber
  • 👕 Shopping: Clothes, shoes, online orders
  • 🎬 Entertainment: OTT platforms, movies, games, outings
  • 💊 Health: Medicines, doctor visits, gym membership
  • 💳 EMIs: Phone, bike, personal loan installments

Add all of these together = your Kul Kharcha (Total Expenses)

What is Kul Bachat (Total Savings)?

Kul Bachat (Total Savings) is the money that remains after subtracting your total expenses from your total income.

📐 Core Financial Formula
Kul Bachat = Kul Aay − Kul Kharcha
₹8,000 = ₹30,000 − ₹22,000
If Kul Bachat is zero or negative — your expenses are exceeding your income. Take action immediately!

Key Financial Formulas Using "Kul"

  • Kul Bachat = Kul Aay − Kul Kharcha
  • Kul Kharcha = Essential Expenses + Wants + EMIs
  • Kul Aay = Salary + Freelance + All Other Sources
  • Savings % = (Kul Bachat ÷ Kul Aay) × 100
  • Expense % = (Kul Kharcha ÷ Kul Aay) × 100
  • Ideal Rule: Kul Kharcha ≤ 80% of Kul Aay

How to Grow Your Kul Bachat – 6 Easy Ways

  • 1

    Save First, Spend What Remains

    The moment your salary arrives, immediately transfer 20% of your Kul Aay to a savings account. Then spend from what's left. "I'll save whatever is left at the end" almost always results in saving nothing — because nothing is ever left.

  • 2

    Set a Monthly Kul Kharcha Limit

    At the start of each month, decide: "My total Kul Kharcha this month will not exceed ₹X." Having a specific number creates accountability and makes you think twice before every non-essential purchase throughout the month.

  • 3

    Cut Non-Essential Expenses by 20–30%

    Eating out, OTT subscriptions, shopping — reducing these non-essential expenses even slightly makes a huge difference to Kul Bachat. Cooking at home instead of ordering delivery saves ₹2,000–4,000 every single month.

  • 4

    Track Small Daily Expenses

    A ₹50 tea, a ₹100 auto ride — these add up to ₹3,000–5,000 in Kul Kharcha every month. Enter them in PaisaTrack daily. The moment you start tracking, you naturally become more careful and Kul Bachat improves on its own.

  • 5

    Cancel Unused Subscriptions

    Netflix, Spotify, gym memberships, cloud storage — how many do you actually use every week? Cancel anything you're not using regularly. This single step reduces Kul Kharcha by ₹500–2,000 every month with zero effort.

  • 6

    Check Your Kul Kharcha Every Week

    Every Sunday, spend 10 minutes reviewing your spending for the week. Catching an overspend in week 1 gives you 3 weeks to course-correct. Waiting until month-end is always too late to fix anything.

The 50/30/20 Rule for Kul Kharcha

If you're not sure how to split your income, the 50/30/20 rule is the simplest and most effective formula:

Example for ₹30,000 monthly income:

  • ₹15,000 (50%) → Essential Kharcha: Rent, groceries, bills, transport
  • ₹9,000 (30%) → Wants Kharcha: Eating out, OTT, shopping, outings
  • ₹6,000 (20%) → Kul Bachat: Bank savings, SIP, emergency fund

Common Mistakes When Calculating "Kul"

  • ❌

    Not including cash expenses in Kul Kharcha: UPI and card transactions appear in statements, but cash payments — tea, auto rides, vegetable vendors — get forgotten. These silently add ₹2,000–4,000 to your real Kul Kharcha every month.

  • ❌

    Not including all income in Kul Aay: Many people count only their main salary. But freelance earnings, interest income, and cashback are all part of Kul Aay and must be included for an accurate financial picture.

  • ❌

    Excluding EMIs from Kul Kharcha: All EMIs — phone, bike, personal loan — are 100% part of your monthly Kul Kharcha and must always be included in your total calculation.

  • ❌

    Including savings inside Kul Kharcha: Savings and expenses are two completely separate things. Adding savings into your expense total throws off your entire calculation.

  • ❌

    Forgetting annual irregular expenses: Insurance renewals, car service, festival shopping — divide these by 12 and include that monthly portion in your Kul Kharcha every single month.

Tips to Keep Kul Aay, Kharcha & Bachat on Track

  • Write down your Kul Aay at the start of every month: Note every income source and the amount — this tells you exactly how much you have before any spending begins.
  • Record expenses daily: Enter your kharcha in PaisaTrack every day — the app automatically calculates Kul Kharcha with category charts at month-end.
  • Save first, spend what remains: Transfer at least 20% of Kul Aay to savings the moment your salary arrives — before spending a single rupee.
  • Check Kul Kharcha every week: A 10-minute Sunday review catches overspending early enough to fix it. Monthly reviews are always too late.
  • Follow the 50/30/20 rule: 50% of Kul Aay on needs, 30% on wants, 20% to Kul Bachat.
  • Track your savings percentage monthly: Calculate (Kul Bachat ÷ Kul Aay) × 100. Above 20% — excellent. Below 10% — time to reduce Kul Kharcha.

Whenever you see the word "Kul" in a financial context — it always means Total. Track your Kul Aay, control your Kul Kharcha, and your Kul Bachat will grow automatically every single month.

What is Kulcha in finance?›

How do you calculate Kul Bachat?›
Kul Bachat = Kul Aay − Kul Kharcha. Example: Kul Aay ₹30,000 and Kul Kharcha ₹22,000 → Kul Bachat = ₹8,000. Savings % = (8,000 ÷ 30,000) × 100 = 26.6% — excellent!

The 50/30/20 rule recommends saving at least 20% of your Kul Aay every month. For ₹30,000 income, that means ₹6,000 per month. If 20% feels too much right now, start with 5–10% and increase gradually.
What is the difference between Kul Kharcha and a Budget?›
A Budget is your plan — how much you intend to spend before the month starts. Kul Kharcha is reality — how much you actually spent. A budget keeps Kul Kharcha under control, and tracking Kul Kharcha tells you whether your budget plan worked.
