What Are Total Monthly Expenses?

Your total monthly expenses are the sum of everything you spend money on in a single month. This includes rent, groceries, electricity, petrol, internet, eating out, shopping, entertainment, and any loan EMIs. Add all of these together and you get your total monthly expense figure.

Once you know your total monthly expenses, you can instantly see how much of your income is being spent — and how much is left to save. If your total expenses ≥ income, you are saving nothing — and that needs to change immediately.

The great thing is that the moment you start tracking your monthly expenses, you naturally begin spending less — because you can see exactly where your money is being wasted.

How to Calculate Your Total Monthly Expenses

Calculating your total monthly expenses is simpler than you think. Just follow these 4 steps:

  • 1

    Pull Up Last Month's Bank Statement

    Open your bank account, UPI apps (PhonePe / Google Pay / Paytm), and credit card and look at last month's transaction history. This is the most accurate record of your actual spending. Download it as a PDF or simply scroll through the app.

  • 2

    List Every Debit Transaction

    Write down every transaction where money left your account (debits). Also recall any cash expenses — auto rides, chai stalls, vegetable vendors — and note them separately. This step is where most people discover hidden spending they never noticed.

  • 3

    Group Expenses by Category

    Sort all your expenses into the categories listed above. For example: Swiggy = Food, Petrol = Transport, Amazon = Shopping. This grouping instantly reveals which category is eating the most money — and that's usually where you can cut back the most.

  • 4

    Add Everything Up = Total Monthly Expenses!

    Add up the totals from all categories. That final number is your total monthly expense. Now subtract it from your income — whatever remains is your savings. If the number is zero or negative, you're spending more than you earn. Time to act!

💡 Simple Formula

Total Monthly Expenses = All Needs Spending + All Wants Spending + EMIs
Monthly Savings = Total Income − Total Monthly Expenses
If your savings number is 0 or negative — it's time to take control right now!

How to Reduce Monthly Expenses – 6 Easy Ways

Reducing expenses doesn't mean depriving yourself — it means spending smarter. Here are 6 proven ways to lower your total monthly expenses:

  • 1

    Separate Needs from Wants

    Look at every expense and ask: "Is this truly necessary?" Rent, food, electricity — necessary. Netflix, dining out, a new phone — not necessary. Cutting just 20–30% of your "wants" spending can free up thousands of rupees every single month.

  • 2

    Pay Attention to Small Daily Expenses

    A ₹50 tea here, a ₹100 auto there, a ₹200 snack somewhere else — these feel tiny, but they add up to ₹3,000–5,000 every month. Once you start tracking these, you'll be genuinely surprised at where your money has been quietly disappearing.

  • 3

    Audit Your Subscriptions

    How many active subscriptions do you have on your phone? Netflix, Amazon Prime, Spotify, gym membership, cloud storage — how many do you actually use regularly? Cancel the ones you don't. This single step can save ₹500–2,000 every month with minimal effort.

  • 4

    Shop for Groceries Smarter

    Make a grocery list at the start of the month and stick to it. Shopping without a list almost always results in buying extra items you didn't need. Buy in bulk for things you use regularly, and take advantage of offers — but only for things that will genuinely get used.

  • 5

    Cut Back on Eating Out

    Food delivery apps and restaurants are one of the biggest expense categories for most people. Cooking at home costs 3–5 times less than ordering from Zomato or Swiggy. Try reducing eating out from 4–5 times a week to just once or twice — the monthly savings will be significant.

  • 6

    Track Your Expenses Every Week

    Just having a budget isn't enough — you need to actively track it. Set aside 10 minutes every Sunday to review your spending for the week. Use a free app like PaisaTrack that automatically calculates your total monthly expenses with visual charts, making tracking effortless.

The 50/30/20 Rule – The Easiest Budget Formula

If you're not sure how much to spend in each category, follow the 50/30/20 rule — the world's most popular and beginner-friendly budgeting formula:

The 50/30/20 Budget Rule

50%
Needs
Rent, Food, Bills, Medicine, Transport
30%
Wants
Eating Out, OTT, Shopping, Entertainment
20%
Savings
Emergency Fund, SIP, Future Goals

Example: If your monthly income is ₹25,000:

  • ₹12,500 (50%) → Needs: Rent, groceries, electricity, transport
  • ₹7,500 (30%) → Wants: Swiggy, Netflix, shopping, outings
  • ₹5,000 (20%) → Savings: Bank savings, SIP, emergency fund

Common Mistakes People Make with Monthly Expenses

  • ❌

    Not tracking cash spending: UPI and card transactions show up in statements, but cash payments — auto rides, local shops, tea stalls — are invisible. These hidden cash expenses often total ₹2,000–4,000 every month.

  • ❌

    Forgetting irregular expenses: Birthday gifts, festival shopping, annual car service, insurance renewals — these come once or twice a year. Divide them by 12 and include that amount in your monthly budget so they don't catch you off guard.

  • ❌

    Ignoring shared or split expenses: Bills split with a roommate, money paid on behalf of a friend who will reimburse you — all of this is still part of your monthly cash flow and should be tracked properly.

  • ❌

    Not tracking credit card spending in real time: Credit card expenses feel like "future money," which leads to overspending. But the bill always arrives. Track credit card transactions in real time, not just when the statement comes.

  • ❌

    Excluding EMIs from total expenses: Many people think of EMIs as "fixed" and separate from their regular expenses. But phone, bike, and furniture EMIs are absolutely part of your total monthly expenses and must be included.

Pro Tips to Spend Less Every Month

  • Save first, spend later: The moment your salary arrives, transfer at least 20% to your savings account immediately. Never save "whatever is left at the end of the month" — because usually nothing is left.
  • Do a weekly spending check-in: Every Sunday, spend 10 minutes reviewing your expenses for the week. Monthly reviews are too infrequent — problems get caught much earlier with weekly check-ins.
  • Follow the 30-day rule for big purchases: Before buying anything over ₹2,000, wait 30 days. If you still want it after 30 days, it may be worth buying. Most of the time, the urge passes.
  • Review your UPI history weekly: Open PhonePe or Google Pay, go to your transaction history, and actually read through it. This simple habit makes people far more aware and intentional about their spending.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What are total monthly expenses? ›
Total monthly expenses are the complete amount of money you spend across all categories in a single month — including rent, food, bills, transport, shopping, entertainment, and EMIs. Add up every category and you have your total monthly expense figure.
How much should my total monthly expenses be? ›
Ideally, your total monthly expenses should not exceed 80% of your income, so you can save at least 20%. The 50/30/20 rule is a great guideline: spend 50% on needs, 30% on wants, and save 20%.
What is the easiest way to track monthly expenses? ›
The easiest way is to use a free app like PaisaTrack. Enter your expenses daily and the app automatically calculates your total monthly spending by category with charts. Alternatively, a simple notebook where you write every expense daily works perfectly too.
Can I reduce expenses even with a low income? ›
Absolutely. Budgeting is even more important when income is limited. Focus on small daily expenses, cancel unused subscriptions, and cook at home more often. These small changes alone can free up ₹2,000–5,000 every month regardless of your income level.
Should I include credit card expenses in my monthly total? ›
Yes, always. Everything purchased on a credit card eventually comes out of your money. Track credit card spending in real time to avoid bill surprises and to get a true picture of your total monthly expenses.
What is the difference between household expenses and monthly budget? ›
Household expenses typically cover just your home-related costs (rent, groceries, utility bills). A monthly budget is a broader plan that includes your total income, all expenses across every category, and your savings goals. Total monthly expenses = household expenses + personal expenses + EMIs.
